1 · Journey map

Six doors, four FACE stages, four steps: how they fit

Three different things were being read as one. FACE is the methodology, the four things a founder can do with a business (Fund, Acquire, Consolidate, Exit). The six doors are the questions founders arrive with, each tagged with the FACE stage it serves. The four steps are the value stream every engagement moves along, from a free diagnostic to a full Readiness Project. A founder picks a door; the door's assessment places them on the value stream; the value stream runs through whichever FACE stages the business needs.

Where the line sits: four layers, and software stops after the first

1 · Automated readiness reportScore, band, written report and a suggested service, produced in the browser from the founder's answers. Says "readiness only, not advice" on every page. Never mentions an investor by name.
2 · Adviser recommendationMade by a named person on the Readiness Call. Confirms or overrides the automated suggestion. The founder is told this on the result: "an adviser confirms the next step on the Readiness Call".
3 · EngagementRoadmap, Insourcing or a Readiness Project, led by David as an officer of the client company. This is where preparation happens.
4 · IntroductionsConsidered and made offline, through the authorised transaction partner, only when the adviser judges the business ready. The platform never matches founders to investors, never promotes an opportunity, never shares founder data with an investor.

Tracking: the one decision

The site stores nothing on the visitor's device and runs no analytics, which is why it needs no cookie banner. Three of Marcus's four measures are already available without changing that:

  • Completions and results: every gated completion writes the score, band and page to the leads table, so completions and result distributions are a query away. The three open self-assessments write nothing, by design.
  • Booked conversations: GoHighLevel records the assessment code, score, band and campaign source on every booking made from an assessment page.
  • Starts: not measured today. Nothing knows a founder has begun answering.

Three options, in order of preference:

  1. Measure what is already there (completions, results, bookings) and accept that starts are unknown. No change, no banner.
  2. An anonymous start count: when the first answer is selected, the page sends a single event (page name only, no identifier, nothing stored on the device) to a server-side function that increments a counter. This gives a start-to-completion rate without tracking anyone, and no banner is needed. It is still a change to the "no analytics" rule and needs Madeleine's and David's agreement before it is built.
  3. Full analytics with a cookie banner. Not recommended: it changes the visitor's first experience of the site for a number that option 2 provides.
