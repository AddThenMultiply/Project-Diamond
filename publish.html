Be findable by the people who decide

You know more about your market than almost anyone in it. The trouble is that the people deciding whether to back you or buy you cannot see it. The Publish stream builds your body of work from what you already say to clients every week — so that when an investor or a buyer goes looking, there is something worth finding.

Take the free ten-minute self-assessment ↓ — it tells you where you actually need help, and who should take the call.

Who this is for

The expert nobody has heard of

Your clients would follow you anywhere. Outside that circle, there is no trace of what you know.

The founder preparing to raise

Before the first meeting, an investor will search your name. What they find sets the tone for everything that follows.

The founder who "doesn't do content"

You have tried, it felt like noise, and it stopped. The problem was the method, not you.

The problem

When a buyer or an investor searches for you, there is little to find. So every relationship starts from zero: your credibility has to be rebuilt in every room, the pitch has to carry all the weight, and the warm introduction you worked for lands cold. In a funding round that costs you the benefit of the doubt. In a sale it costs you the premium a buyer pays for a founder who is known in the market, because a known founder brings a known business. Invisible expertise is priced as if it did not exist.

What we do

A ninety-day sprint that turns the expertise you already spend on clients into published work — without inventing a persona, a platform or a publishing habit you will not keep.

What you walk away with

  • Published authority that supports the pitch. When your story is told in the room, the evidence for it is already out there, in your name, where the listener can find it afterwards.
  • Published authority that supports the price. A founder who is known in the market brings a business that is known in the market — and the valuation conversation starts from a different place.
  • A body of work that keeps growing. One format, one rhythm, one point of view: a habit that survives the sprint and keeps compounding after it.
