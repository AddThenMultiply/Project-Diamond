Pitch · Key Person of Influence · feeds Fund and Exit

A story that agrees with the numbers

At some point you will have to explain the business to someone who can say no: an investor, a buyer, an acquirer's board. The Pitch stream builds the version of your story that survives that room — anchored in your own diagnostic numbers, and deliverable by your team, not just by you.

Who this is for

The founder about to raise

You know the business inside out, and that is the problem: you have never had to make it simple for someone who does not.

The founder preparing to sell

A buyer will hear your story once from you, then test it against the data room. The two have to say the same thing.

The team where only the founder can tell it

If the story stops when you leave the room, so does the deal. Investors and buyers notice.

The problem

In a funding round, the story and the numbers disagree. The narrative promises one kind of growth; the model shows another; the investor prices the gap and calls it risk. In a sale, the pitch can only be delivered by the founder — so every meeting, every management presentation and every follow-up depends on one person's diary and one person's energy. A buyer reads that as key-person dependence and discounts for it. Either way, a good business is sold short by the way it is explained.

What we do

A focused ninety-day sprint, run alongside your other readiness work. Everything is rehearsed against your own diagnostic numbers, so the story and the data never drift apart.

What you walk away with

  • A pitch the whole team can deliver. The same story, at ninety seconds and at one line, in the mouths of the people who will be in the room after you.
  • A deck outline. The running order and the evidence each slide needs, ready for your designer and reconciled to your model.
  • The narrative that anchors the data room. The investor and buyer versions, written down, so every document a diligence team opens tells the same story you told them.

Where it fits

Pitch is one of the influence assets from the Key Person of Influence method, and it feeds two phases of FACE. It sharpens the Fund conversation, because a fundable founder is one who can explain the business in the investor's terms. And it sharpens the Exit conversation, because a sellable business is one whose story does not depend on the seller. In the value stream, the narrative work usually starts once the diagnostic has given you numbers to anchor to, runs alongside the roadmap, and lands in the data room during the Readiness Project.
